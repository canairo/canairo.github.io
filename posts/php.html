Intro

It's important to be cautious. There are two things that are true, in these uncertain, flimsy times:

  1. Frontier models are now finding vulnerabilities in systems at absurd rates.
  2. If you don't know what you're doing, you will be lied to.

This is why, when php2shell and node2shell dropped, a whole slew of slopped-up exploits emerged, none of which worked. I think the way in which they did not work is incredibly funny and worth talking about, and it's also good to keep your senses sharp so you don't get deceived.

On a personal note I find this sort of charlatanry interesting. It's, like, second-order charlatanry; the average person who publishes these slop exploits does not even know that they do not work -- the agents are convincing liars. They produce reams and reams of documentation and exploit sciripts coolly and affirmatively asserting that their exploit works, and to the untrained (or uncaring, or unconcerned) eye it really does seem like the exploits work.
